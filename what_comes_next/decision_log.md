# Question 3: Make the Avatar the Center of Social Interaction

## Connect Avatars, Emotes, Throwables, and Chat

The core decision is to let the player's avatar perform their social interactions.
Emotes express the character's mood. Throwables become actions the character directs at another player.
Chat connects words to that same character through quick messages with a chosen emote.
Together, these give the avatar a personality that players can use at the table.

The prototype demonstrates this through Momo, who performs full-body expressions, leaves its frame to visit an opponent, and reacts alongside a quick message.
The intended value is a stronger connection between player identity and self-expression.
Choosing an avatar can shape how a player communicates, including its movement, humor, and interactions with other characters.

## Make Expression Fast and Personal

The desktop-client context makes keyboard shortcuts a useful part of the experience.
Players can quickly trigger an emote, direct a throwable, or send a quick message during a hand.
Typing also opens chat directly.

Players can write their own quick messages and choose which emote accompanies each one, or keep a message text-only.
The same character can express different personalities through the combinations players create.
The opportunity is to give players room for their own jokes and reactions, with controls fast enough to use during play.

Future avatar customization could let players shape their character's face, choose hairstyles, and adjust colors.
The same custom character would perform emotes, use throwables, and express player-written messages, connecting appearance with personality.
An initial version could offer preset feature combinations that remain expressive during animation, with freeform face sculpting explored later.
This is a future concept beyond the current prototype.

## Let the Receiving Avatar Participate

A future extension is to animate the avatar receiving a throwable.
The current prototype focuses on the sender. The recipient has basic impact feedback, but does not yet perform a character response to being hit.

Giving the recipient its own reaction would turn the exchange into a performance between two characters.
The response could depend on the incoming action and the receiving avatar, creating room for recognizable relationships and unexpected moments.

## Extend the Idea Through Characters and IP Collaborations

Distinctive characters could bring their own expressions, signature actions, and interactions with specific opponents.
Possible future concepts include:

- Luffy expressing anger by unleashing Gum-Gum Gatling at an opponent's avatar.
- Iron Man using a palm repulsor as a throwable action.
- Naruto performing a special version of an action when the target is Sasuke, creating an Easter egg tied to that pairing.
- Batman responding with "I'm Batman" whether the selected emote is happy or sad, making his personality the joke.

These are future concepts, not implemented features or confirmed collaborations.
The combination of character-specific performances and player-written messages creates room for both authored content and player creativity.
Whether that leads to more social participation or lasting attachment to an avatar remains a product hypothesis to validate.
